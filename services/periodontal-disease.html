Periodontal Disease Treatment

Catching gum disease early protects both your smile and your whole-body health.

What Is Periodontal Disease?

Periodontal (gum) disease starts quietly — bleeding when you brush, slight redness, or gums that seem to be pulling away from your teeth. Left untreated, it progresses from gingivitis to periodontitis, where the infection spreads below the gumline and begins to break down the bone and tissue that hold your teeth in place.

At Stunning Smiles, we take gum health seriously because of its ripple effect on the rest of your body. Research links periodontal disease to heart disease, diabetes complications, and even cognitive decline. Treating it early isn't just about saving teeth — it's about protecting your overall health.

How We Treat Gum Disease

Early Detection

Periodontal charting and digital X-rays at every cleaning so we catch bone loss and pocket depth changes before they become serious problems.

Scaling & Root Planing

A deep cleaning below the gumline that removes bacterial plaque and tartar, smooths root surfaces, and lets inflamed gum tissue heal.

Non-Surgical Therapy

Ozone and antimicrobial rinses to reduce harmful bacteria, used alongside deep cleaning for a gentler, biocompatible approach wherever possible.

Ongoing Maintenance

More frequent periodontal maintenance visits (rather than standard six-month cleanings) to keep the disease from returning or progressing.

What to Expect at Your Visit

  1. Periodontal evaluation — We measure gum pocket depths, check for bleeding and recession, and review your digital X-rays for bone loss.
  2. Diagnosis & staging — We explain exactly how advanced your gum disease is and what that means for your treatment plan.
  3. Deep cleaning (if needed) — Scaling and root planing is typically done in sections, often with local anesthesia for comfort.
  4. Home care plan — Specific brushing, flossing, and rinsing guidance tailored to your gum health, not a generic handout.
  5. Maintenance schedule — A follow-up cadence designed to keep your gums healthy long-term.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common signs include bleeding when you brush or floss, persistent bad breath, gums that look red or swollen, and teeth that feel loose or look longer than before. Many people have no pain at all in the early stages, which is why regular checkups matter.

We numb the treatment area beforehand, so most patients feel pressure rather than pain. Some mild sensitivity afterward is normal and typically resolves within a few days.

Early-stage gingivitis is often fully reversible with professional cleaning and better home care. More advanced periodontitis can't be completely reversed, but it can be stopped and managed so it doesn't progress further.
